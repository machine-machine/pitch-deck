## The Core Thesis

Every major enterprise cost center has the same structure:

```
High volume + repetitive core
+ Occasional complex exception requiring judgment
+ Measurable output quality
```

Single AI agents fail on complex exceptions. A multi-specialist AI organization with hierarchical reasoning handles both — and improves over time.

This is what MachineMachine builds.

## Cost Centers Ranked by AI Org ROI

### 🔴 Tier 1 — Highest ROI (accelerate now)

#### 1. Software Engineering
- **Typical spend**: 15–25% of OpEx at tech companies
- **Pain**: Code review, testing, documentation, incident response, on-call rotation
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★★
  - Our benchmark (incident response protocol) IS software engineering org work
  - Specialists: Systems Architect, Coordination, Governance, Emergence, Network Analyst
  - Market signal: GitHub Copilot → $1.5B ARR in 2 years (single-agent)
  - Multi-agent org unlocks what single-agent Copilot can't: cross-domain synthesis
- **Measurable output**: Code shipped, bugs caught, MTTR (mean time to resolution)

#### 2. Customer Support / Service
- **Typical spend**: 10–20% of OpEx
- **Pain**: 80% of tickets are repetitive; human escalation for edge cases is expensive
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★☆
  - Star topology: intake → triage specialist → domain specialist → QA → response
  - Org learns which ticket types need which specialists
  - Measurable: resolution time, deflection rate, CSAT score
- **Enterprise prize**: $10–50M/year for mid-size companies

#### 3. IT Operations / Security
- **Typical spend**: 8–15% of OpEx
- **Pain**: On-call ($300K+/engineer/year), alert fatigue, patch compliance, audit prep
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★★
  - HRM architecture is native here: coordinator escalates to specialists, loops until resolved
  - Our exact benchmark task (incident response) = IT ops
  - Multi-specialist org catches what single-agent monitoring misses
- **Measurable output**: MTTR, alert resolution rate, compliance pass rate

### 🟡 Tier 2 — High ROI, 6–18 month horizon

#### 4. Legal & Compliance
- **Typical spend**: 3–8% of OpEx (but $1K+/hr lawyers)
- **Pain**: Contract review, regulatory monitoring, policy drafting, audit prep
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★☆
  - Governance Designer role already in our org spec
  - Requires human-in-loop for final sign-off (risk tolerance low)
  - Prize: in-house legal at Fortune 500 = $50–150M/year
- **Constraint**: Hallucination risk is high-stakes; needs verification layer

#### 5. Finance & Reporting
- **Typical spend**: 4–8% of OpEx
- **Pain**: 60–70% of analyst time = data wrangling, not analysis
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★☆
  - Pipeline topology: data → analysis → narrative → review
  - Direct link to DEXY/Aether: same quant reasoning infrastructure
  - FP&A, month-end close, variance analysis, board reporting
- **Measurable output**: Report turnaround time, accuracy, analyst hours saved

#### 6. HR / Talent Operations
- **Typical spend**: 5–10% of OpEx
- **Pain**: JD writing, CV screening, onboarding, performance review drafts, L&D content
- **AI org fit**: ★★★☆☆
  - High volume, low variance — textbook AI org territory
  - Risk: bias and compliance scrutiny (needs careful design)
- **Constraint**: Regulatory exposure high; slower enterprise sales cycle

---

### 🟢 Tier 3 — Strategic, longer horizon

#### 7. Sales Operations
- **Typical spend**: 2–5% of OpEx, tied to 100% of revenue
- **Pain**: Lead scoring, proposal generation, CRM hygiene, competitive intel
- **AI org fit**: ★★★☆☆
  - Pipeline org: research → qualify → personalize → follow-up
  - 1% conversion improvement = outsized revenue impact
- **Note**: High sensitivity, humans want control here

#### 8. Procurement
- **Typical spend**: 3–6% of OpEx
- **Pain**: Vendor evaluation, RFP responses, spend analysis, contract prep
- **AI org fit**: ★★★★☆
  - Pure information synthesis — ideal multi-specialist task
  - Very underserved by current AI tooling (greenfield opportunity)
- **Note**: Long sales cycles in enterprise procurement teams

#### 9. Marketing / Content
- **Typical spend**: 3–8% of OpEx
- **Pain**: Single-agent content = low quality; org adds research → copy → brand check
- **AI org fit**: ★★★☆☆
  - Already commoditized at single-agent level
  - MachineMachine's growth flywheel (benchmark → blog → LinkedIn) IS this
- **Note**: Differentiate on org quality, not "AI content" (crowded market)
